| G-036 | The commercial packaging in COST-MODEL.md §6.1 is approved (Team $299 / Growth $999 / Enterprise BYOC); concurrency is licensed capacity, not a metered quantity, leaving three metered meters | Owner approval 10 October 2026, on research against Stripe Billing Meters, Lago, OpenMeter, Metronome, Fly.io, Netlify, Databricks, Railway, Render and AWS Quotas. The load-bearing correction: `concurrency_peak` cannot be billed as a monthly quantity because the provider's `last` aggregation returns the final event of the billing period — 720 hourly samples would bill one hour, not the peak — and no `max` aggregation exists (confirmed against the meters API and Metronome's migration table). Every vendor that sells concurrency (Fly.io soft/hard limits, Netlify per-unit add-on, Databricks fixed quota, Lambda provisioned concurrency) sells a limit, never an accumulation, so concurrency becomes a licensed per-slot charge enforced at admission by `internal/quota`, with the observed peak used only for alerts. Our ledger is the source of truth and provider aggregates are a disposable projection: rollups recomputed not incremented, corrections as referencing events, `identifier` equal to our ledger row ID because provider dedup covers only ~24h against a 34-day retry horizon, timestamps read from the ledger and never from the wall clock. No environment or generation dimensions in provider payloads (100 combinations per customer is a small budget). Minimum billable quantum of 5 minutes per generation applied in our ledger, not by provider period-total rounding, which would let one short environment absorb another's remainder. Graduated overage only; volume tiers plus thresholds make invoice totals fall as usage grows. Spend alerts built in-house because the provider's are preview-gated. Failure attribution follows the observed industry rule (Netlify): consumed capacity on customer-side gate failures bills, platform-side admission failures do not. Interview validation of willingness-to-pay is explicitly **not** part of this approval and stays a pre-launch task under G7.12; the known unhedged risk is that the $4,515 floor needs ~15 Team customers per fleet or BYOC |
| G-037 | Commercial policy confirmed: sandbox stays deferred behind the write-access-only posture; concurrency is licensed capacity at 5 slots (Team) / 20 (Growth); the trial is a bounded freeze; seats stay but are never punitive; hosted viability is stated as ~15 Team customers per fleet or BYOC | Owner approval 10 October 2026, resolving the five open commercial forks against the G-036 evidence. **Sandbox:** the deferral stands rather than buying nested-virt capacity now — the residual (the build path executes PR source unsandboxed) is already published in the limitation matrix, and a managed sandbox provider was rejected because it puts a vendor in the trust boundary that G-030 deliberately keeps credential-free. Shared multi-tenant foundations were considered and rejected as contradicting G-004. Acceptance of willingness-to-pay remains outstanding and is a G7.12 pre-launch task |

## Critical qualifications

G-004 cannot proceed to production use until PostgreSQL, Kafka, Temporal, Redis, S3 and identity boundaries pass sibling-preview tests. Untrusted previews use dedicated bounded PostgreSQL/Redis by default; reviewed internal-only shared pools are a separate qualified profile. Where a broker/workflow service cannot enforce necessary authorization and resource limits, that dependency moves to a dedicated instance/profile or qualified trusted gateway. G-005 requires security review of privileged chaos components. G-007 requires an observed Spot-shortage fallback, not a YAML demonstration. G-009 requires independent gate sign/write permissions and exact current-head checks.

G-021 is deferred from the 1.0 critical path together with G-029, and is not waived. Its exit evidence — measured per-pod host overhead, nested-virtualization CPU penalty, guest-kernel verification, a named adversarial escape corpus and a guest-kernel patch SLA — remains required before any candidate outside a bound repository is admitted. G-029's re-entry trigger is the governing condition, not a date.
